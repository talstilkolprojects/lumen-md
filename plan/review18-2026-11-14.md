### The four changes, from reading

- **(1a)** `evictionOrder` (`roomQuota.mjs:221-238`) adds up each owner value's bytes, for the address and for the /48 (`:232`).
  - Rooms of an owner past the cap skip the protection filter and sort first, largest first.
  - Writes are never refused, which keeps a user's edits safe.
- **(1b)** A return protects nothing when the joiner matches the creator's address or the creator's /48. This is checked both in the room (`worker.mjs:265`) and in `joined` (`:722`).
- **(1c)** The multi-room plan sorts in descending order (`roomQuota.mjs:270`), still at most 32 rooms. Tiny rooms no longer block it.
- **(2)** `MAX_ASKS = 256` (`worker.mjs:93`). Every `canEvict` call counts, and a plan stops at its first refusal.

### The griefing questions

**Driving a victim's protection off by growing their bytes.** Writing to a room needs its token.
- **An outsider can't aim at a victim.** The owner value is an HMAC under the server secret. An attacker filling its own owner values only strips protection from creators who happen to share one of them, at random.
  - At most about 200 values can be over the cap at once (4 GB / 20 MB), out of 2^20.
- **A co-editor can.** A co-editor holding the token can grow a shared room, and those bytes count against the **creator**. If the creator is near the cap, that pushes them over, and all the creator's rooms lose protection and go first when the store is full. That is RR18-469-1.

**IPv4 rotation (accepted decision).** Another IPv4 address counts as another person.
- Holding the store with protected rooms now takes about 200 owner values with a return every 14 days. At review 11 it took 20 addresses.
- One more case: a creator who created over IPv4 and returns over its own IPv6 counts as another person, because an IPv4 creator has no /48 value.
- That is RR18-469-2. Both are within the stated decision.

**Plan starvation.**
- A plan now picks large rooms first.
- A refusal costs one ask and removes that room for an hour.
- Blocking one admission takes 256 refusing rooms per hour, each holding a socket that sent the token. That is far more expensive than the earlier paths.

**Charging refused attempts:** kept by decision. I found no harm to honest users.

### RR18-469-1 (LOW, from reading): a co-editor's writes count against the creator

- **Where:** `roomQuota.mjs:232` (bytes per creator owner value).
- **Effect:** a co-editor can push a near-cap creator over the cap and strip the protection of all the creator's rooms.
- **Limits:** it needs access to one of the victim's rooms, the victim already near the cap, and a full store before anything is lost.
- **Fix:** accept it, or charge bytes written by someone other than the creator to the writer.

### RR18-469-2 (LOW, accepted): the cost of the remaining DoS

- About 200 owner values held over 14-day cycles, from reading.
- The dual-stack case above.
- No fix; this is the stated decision.

### RR18-451-1 (LOW, from reading): the 500-attempt log can drop the switch-time records

**Where:**
- `airplane.ts:103` and `:160`: the log keeps its last 500 attempts.
- `airplane.ts:799-801`: every CSP refusal is recorded as an attempt.
- `airplaneCertificate.ts:94-103` and `NetworkMonitor.tsx:76`: the certificate and its summary are built from that log.

**Scenario:**
1. The switch goes on with an embed open. Its in-flight FRAME and POPUP records are written.
2. The user then opens a note with more than 500 remote images. Each one is refused and recorded.
3. The switch-time records fall out of the log, and the sealed certificate's summary says `left: 0`.

**Why it is low:**
- `airplaneCertificate.ts:34` discloses that only the last 500 attempts are kept. The lane added that line in this round.
- But the summary carries no sign that records were dropped. A certificate that looks clean is the one thing this feature promises.

**Fix:** never evict attempts that reached the network (pin them), or put a dropped count in the summary.

**Verdict:** approve (low only).
