### S27-1 — LOW — clock skew past `PAIRING_CLOCK_SKEW_MS` reopens S13-1 in one-way pairing

**Setup.** The laptop's clock is 150 s behind A's. It enters the code at 1:40. The laptop confirms, then A confirms (last); A's hold ends at 2:00 + 60 s. The storage holds A's confirmation back. A revokes the phone at 3:05. At 3:35 the storage hands the confirmation over; by the laptop's clock it is then 1:05 after the code was made. Result:
```
pairingKept ends 180000 ms after the code | A revokes at 3:05: epoch 2 | laptop confirm at 3:35: done | verified: true | uploaded: 1 | revoked phone reads: ["a.md","new.md"]
```

**Cause:**
- The new device's window is `min(pair + TTL, issuedAt + TTL)`, measured on its own clock against the issuer's `issuedAt`.
- `readPairingCode` accepts a code up to 60 s in the future *at pair time*. So a new device that is behind by 60 s plus the time since the code was issued still accepts the code, and its window ends that much after A's hold.

**Needed for the attack:** more than 60 s of clock skew, the storage withholding A's confirmation, and A revoking inside the gap. Two-way pairing isn't affected, because each side times its own interval on its own clock and the answer comes after the request.

**Fix (either one):**
- Put the new device's remaining window (its `until` minus its `now`) inside `confirm-joiner`, under the SAS MAC, and set `pairingKept = now + remaining + skew` on the issuer. That is exact in real time, whatever the clocks say.
- Or refuse codes from the future at `pair()`.

### S27-2 — LOW — the hold has no upper bound

**Setup.** A pairing is confirmed while the device's clock is a year fast; NTP then corrects it. Ten minutes later A cancels and revokes. Result:
```
PairingPendingError until +365 days
```

**Cause:** `checkNoPairingPending` uses `max(pairingUntil, pairingKept)` with no sanity bound, and by design cancel no longer clears `pairingKept`. So every key change, revocations included, is refused until that time. (A wrong `pairingUntil` was always cleared by cancel.)

**Fix:** treat a hold further ahead than `PAIRING_REQUEST_TTL_MS + PAIRING_CLOCK_SKEW_MS` from now as void, the way `keyChangeBlockedSince` voids future timestamps. Apply it to `pairingUntil` too.

### S27-3 — INFO — `pairingKept` can't be abused

- **Denial of service:**
  - `pairingKept` is set only in `confirmIssuing`, after a valid `confirm-joiner`, that is, after A's user pressed *They match*.
  - Three attempts with a leaked code by someone holding the pairing key left no hold (`kept=none`), and A cancelled and revoked at once (`rr27 (c)`).
  - Each confirmed pairing holds for at most the code's or answer's life + 60 s, and the value is a `max`, not a sum.
- **A revoked device** can't set it:
  - it lives in local state only;
  - after a revocation the device lacks the current pairing key, so its reveal and confirm messages don't open (they're checked against `keys.epoch` and the pairing key).
- **The cost of the fix, as documented:** after a confirmed pairing, the existing device can't revoke for up to 3 minutes (code) or 6 minutes (answer), cancel or not. The pre-SAS "cancel, then revoke at once" escape is gone once the user has pressed *They match* on it. That is the right trade.

### Cancel racing the final confirm — safe in both orders

The race was on one device: A's cancel and A's final confirm.

| Order | Outcome | A's revocation right after |
|---|---|---|
| Cancel first | A's confirm aborts (`PairingAbortedError`); the laptop keeps waiting and is never verified | goes through (epoch 2) |
| Confirm first | A posts `confirm-issuer` and holds `pairingKept`; the cancel leaves the hold; the laptop commits | refused (`PairingPendingError`) |

- On one engine the two calls are serialised by `exclusive`. Across tabs they are serialised by the device lock, since confirm, reject and cancel now take it.
- A saves `pairingKept` *before* it posts `confirm-issuer`, so a crash between the two leaves a hold with no confirmation, which is safe.
- A key change waiting on the lock while a confirm runs either sees the hold or ends the issuing session through `liveIssuing`.
